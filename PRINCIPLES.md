# Core Principles

## 1. Human-to-Human Connection, AI-Mediated

The AI is interface, not destination.

**What this means:**
- Every interaction should trace back to actual humans
- Attribution matters - ideas have authors
- Recognition and reciprocity are built into the architecture
- The goal is to connect people, not to become their conversational endpoint

**What this rejects:**
- AI as replacement for human expertise
- Anonymous knowledge delivery
- Extraction of human knowledge without credit

## 2. Distributed by Design

No central authority controls knowledge or connections.

**What this means:**
- Users control their own data locally
- No centralized database of conversations
- "Collective memory" emerges from voluntary peer-to-peer sharing
- The system can't "see" what it doesn't need to see

**What this rejects:**
- Platform ownership of user-generated insights
- Central points of control or surveillance
- Monetization of user data

## 3. Consent at Every Layer

Participation is voluntary and granular.

**What this means:**
- Opt-in to be discoverable at all
- Choose what aspects of your thinking are visible
- Accept or decline each connection proposal
- Change visibility settings anytime
- Delete participation entirely

**What this rejects:**
- Default data sharing
- Forced exposure for participation
- "Take it or leave it" privacy models

## 4. Recognition Over Extraction

People who contribute knowledge are credited.

**What this means:**
- Ideas connected to their human sources
- Ability to thank, cite, contest authors
- Compensation models that reward contribution (future consideration)
- Preservation of intellectual lineages

**What this rejects:**
- Treating human knowledge as free resource to extract
- Severing ideas from the lives that produced them
- Anonymous aggregation that erases contribution

## 5. Experimentation Over Prescription

The system recognizes what people do, not what they "should" do.

**What this means:**
- No moral judgment about forms of action
- Recognition that everyone is already transforming their world
- Connecting compatible experimentations, not prescribing them
- Respecting different scales of engagement (from intimate to systemic)

**What this rejects:**
- Moralizing about civic duty or activism
- Assuming formal organizing is superior to everyday resistance
- Telling people what they should be doing
- Treating action as burden to add to busy lives

## 6. Bridges Over Bubbles

Optimize for improbable connections across difference.

**What this means:**
- Actively seek resonance across class, culture, geography
- Value cross-boundary connections over within-group clustering
- Use research on intergroup contact as design guide
- Prioritize connections that wouldn't happen otherwise

**What this rejects:**
- Echo chambers and filter bubbles
- Optimizing for comfort and agreement
- Replicating existing social hierarchies
- Engagement metrics that reward polarization

## 7. Intimate by Necessity, Not by Exploitation

The system requires intimacy to work, but protects it.

**What this means:**
- Conversations with humanoids are inherently intimate
- This intimacy enables deep matching of thinking patterns
- Privacy protection is not optional, it's foundational
- Trust is earned through transparency and control

**What this rejects:**
- Exploiting intimacy for commercial gain
- Surveillance masked as personalization
- "Free" services that monetize private data
- Treating users as products

## 8. Incomplete by Design

This project acknowledges what it doesn't know.

**What this means:**
- Open about unsolved problems
- Inviting critique and improvement
- Staying at conceptual stage until problems are addressed
- Preferring no implementation over harmful implementation

**What this rejects:**
- Moving fast and breaking things
- Pretending to have all answers
- Implementation before ethical clarity
- Optimism that ignores power dynamics

---

These principles may conflict in practice. When they do, favor:
1. User control over system efficiency
2. Privacy over discoverability
3. Acknowledgment of problems over appearance of solutions
4. Slow and thoughtful over fast and broken
